How to read this file:

- A plain card ID marks a decision: Daniel answered that card, and it binds. An `edit` answer is restated in his words; an `accept` answer binds the proposal as it stood. Where a later answer changes an earlier one, the later one wins and both IDs are named; [RULES.md](RULES.md#authority) lists the known cases.
- **Open** marks a proposal, not a decision. It names its card and who decides it.
- **Open (agents):** a technical or implementation detail. The migration agent may decide it, consistent with every decision, and records its choice in the PR.
- **Open (Daniel):** a value, feature, design or business choice. Agents may build the proposal on `dev` as a declared feature or design change, and it reaches `prod` only after Daniel accepts it in the before-and-after report (DEL-06, DEL-69). Website changes merge into `dev` like any other change and show on the hidden preview; only going public waits for his go (DEL-06, amended 2026-10-27).
- Outward actions wait for Daniel's explicit go, even when their card is accepted. [DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md#outward-actions-waiting-for-daniels-go) lists them in one place.
- By default, Daniel decides open cards in parts 01, 04a to 04d and 06 and the non-technical cards of part 08; agents decide open cards in parts 02, 03, 05 and 07 and part 08's technical cross-part cards. A card that spends money, publishes something or changes what Daniel reviews stays his. The 70 cards raised to key on 2026-10-26 are next for Daniel, in the order of [review-queue.md](review-queue.md).

## What counts as evidence

- Passing tests alone never establish design quality (INT-67). A review agent on the model Daniel uses at the time (Opus, Codex or another) judges visual and motion quality from rendered output on a phone and a desktop, against INT-12's bar: charts that withstand side-by-side comparison with Highcharts and ECharts, typography and layout against FT-grade and Wall Street Journal editorial design, and animation that is delightful and smooth, "the whimsical quality of Studio Ghibli meets the polish of Apple's website animations". Its verdict reaches Daniel in the before-and-after report before `prod` (INT-12, INT-67, DEL-68, DEL-69). Daniel can adjust visual and motion quality there, independently of the rest.
- The process is evidence, the individual tests are not (DEL-68). Agents may delete tests that are no longer needed, and a test audit removes tests that are only formal, test the obvious, repeat each other or test no real functionality. No agent may delete or skip a step of the process: the gates and the adversarial reviews.
- **Open (Daniel):** DEL-81 (later) lets steps, tiers, hooks, reviews and thresholds change when they slow development, while DEL-68 says no agent removes or skips a step of the process. How the two fit is not decided; until Daniel decides, a change to a step is recorded with its reason in the PR that makes it. DEL-81 also runs the long suites and the adversarial reviews per `pre-prod` batch rather than on every change.
- Before `prod`, the full set of tests that already ran before it, in the release run and the full run on `pre-prod`, runs once more on the frozen candidate cut from `pre-prod`, to prove everything still works after many merges combined (DEL-71, DEL-81). Every row of DEL-71's table must pass; a row that cannot run fails the candidate unless [DELIVERY.md](DELIVERY.md#records-kept-here) names it as a standing gap.
- The before-and-after report is the evidence Daniel reads before `prod`: every design and feature change one by one, with crops, clips, its cause and a one-command revert (DEL-69).
- Only release and candidate receipts are evidence (DEL-81, DEL-20, DEL-21). A merge into `dev` rests on the fast gate, which leaves no receipt, and is reported as merged, not as verified.
- **Open (agents):** DEL-21 proposes honest status: only a release or candidate receipt counts as verification; a build, a screenshot, a review verdict or a Linear state never does on its own, and an unrun check is never reported as a pass.
- **Open (agents):** DEL-13 proposes that every check tests the release artifacts the gate built once and hashed.
- **Open (agents):** DEL-35 proposes that the reviews are required steps with advisory verdicts: the adversarial review, run on Opus, reads each `pre-prod` batch before its candidate is frozen, no merge into `dev` waits for a verdict (whether a negative verdict also holds a batch back from `pre-prod`, or a candidate from `prod`, is open), a finding counts once reproduced, and a review that finds nothing proves nothing on its own (DEL-68, DEL-81).
- **Open (agents):** PRF-01 proposes Claw Mini as the reference host for every budget; the fast tier is also judged on Lenovo (OPN-55, DEL-50).
- **Open (agents):** DEL-79 proposes that every accepted item names its checks, or is marked `judged` when a review verdict is its evidence, in a generated coverage file that becomes INT-67's done report.

## Test tiers

There are three tiers (DEL-81, which replaces DEL-71's four): the fast gate in `dev`, the release run on `pre-prod` per batch, and the full run before a candidate is cut, which the prod step repeats on the frozen candidate (DEL-71). The tiers still grow toward `prod`. The commands, budgets and triggers below are proposals (DEL-08, DEL-24, PRF-51; open, agents).

Measured on Claw Mini (Mac mini, Apple M4, 10 cores, 16 GiB, macOS 26.3 build 25D125) at `b4db233` on 2026-10-22 between 03:05 and 03:15 UTC: rustc and cargo 1.99.0, `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4`, `nice -n 10`, one run at a time, 1-minute load 2.6 to 4.4 with other agents active. Every run passed. Lenovo was not measured. The "after an edit" runs touched `richview-chart/src/lib.rs` without changing its content, so their times are lower bounds. The logs stay in the ignored `tmp/lockdown-files-v3/`.
