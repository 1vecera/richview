The service

RichView's hosted server lets you publish the reports your agent writes and open them in a browser. Daniel Vecera operates it as a personal project; his address is in the Impressum. It is free, it is an early release, and it comes without a service level: it can be slow, unavailable or changed, and it may be closed. If it closes, we tell account holders by e-mail at least 30 days before reports are deleted.

Your account

You need an e-mail address you can read. Keep your password and your CLI tokens to yourself; what is published with your token counts as published by you. You can belong to one or more organizations: one is created when you sign up, and a colleague can invite you into theirs. An account created from a shared report alone has no organization until you create or join one. One organization holds up to 50 reports and 50 MiB. You can delete a report at any time, and you can have the account deleted by writing to the address in the Impressum.

Organizations

An organization's owners and admins invite people by e-mail, give them a role and can remove them; an admin cannot invite or remove someone with a higher role than their own. Owners and admins can manage every report in the organization, including private ones. Reports belong to the organization: when you leave it or your account is deleted, the reports you published stay with it under another owner, unless you were its only member, in which case they are deleted with it.

Your reports

Your reports stay yours. You give us only the permission needed to store them and show them to the people they are shared with. A new report is private by default. You, and the owners and admins of the organization it was published in, can share it with named people, open it to everyone in that organization, or switch on its public link, and then anyone with the link can open it. You are responsible for what you publish and for having the right to publish it, including any personal data in it.

What is not allowed

No unlawful content, no malware or phishing, no content that infringes someone else's rights, no attempts to break, overload or probe the service, and no use of it to send unsolicited mail. We may remove a report or suspend an account that does this, and we will say why unless the law prevents it.
